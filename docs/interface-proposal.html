01 · Palette

Colour always means something

The four hues above are reserved for margin, border, padding and content — the code every browser's dev tools already use, which is the code your users will meet next. They appear nowhere else.

Everything else is ink on paper: #191C1A on #FFFFFF, with a cool grey #E8EAE6 ground. Primary buttons, selection edges and focus rings are all ink. No gradient, no brand hue.

Which means the only saturated things on screen are the user's design and the measurements of it. In a design tool that is not a style choice — it's a requirement. Purple chrome makes it impossible to judge your own colours.

02 · Type

Two voices, one family

Plain languageIBM Plex Sans 13/1.5
Values & CSSIBM Plex Mono 11
AnnotationsMono 10, +0.09em, caps
Panel titlesSans 15/500

Plex was drawn for an engineering company and has a mono sibling by the same hand, so the pairing is one decision rather than two.

It does real work: every control reads Space inside in the sans and padding in the mono beneath it. A beginner is never blocked by jargon, and learns the actual word by the fourth time they see it. That single pattern is the whole "beginners and designers" brief, solved typographically.

03 · Layout

Four surfaces, not six

Top railProject, breakpoints, checks, export
LeftInsert / Layers — one panel, toggled
CentreCanvas, the largest thing on screen
RightInspector, ordered as you think

Breakpoints move up out of the status bar — it's the control you reach for most and it belongs beside the thing it changes. The status bar goes entirely; element counts are not information.

The inspector is ordered Content → Box → Appearance → Checks: what it says, then the space around it, then how it looks. Not alphabetically, and not by CSS spec order.

04 · The canvas

A measured drawing

Selecting anything draws its four boxes in place, with a dimension line and real numbers — the way a drawing is dimensioned, not the way a panel reports. You drag the bands directly; the numbers follow.

The selection edge is ink, 1.5px, never a hue — it has to stay legible over whatever the user has designed, including a blue button.

Spacing offers a scale (4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24) rather than a raw number field. Beginners get consistency by default; free entry stays one deliberate click away.

05 · Checks

A conscience, not a report

The rail carries a live count. Problems are stated where they happen, in the words of the consequence rather than the rule: "Screen readers use that order to navigate", not "WCAG 1.3.1".

Every check ends in a verb — Make it Heading 2 — so the fix is one click, not homework. Contrast is shown while you pick the colour, not audited afterwards.

06 · Removed

What goes, and why

  • Gradients — they read as decoration and compete with the user's work.
  • Emoji in the UI — ✅ in a panel is a placeholder that shipped.
  • Shadows on everything — depth now means one thing: this floats above. Panels use hairlines.
  • 16px radii — 4px on controls, 6px on panels. Softness comes from spacing, not corners.
  • Icon-only rails — elements are named. Nobody guesses a glyph for <article>.
  • The status bar — its one useful control moved up; the rest was telemetry.
